Chapter 1: Executive Summary & Comprehensive Scorecard
1.1 Institutional Problem Statement & "Direction Over Price" Thesis
In Indian institutional asset management, particularly for long-only mutual funds managing over ■60 Lakh Crores (720B
USD) in domestic equity schemes, traditional quantitative asset allocation models suffer from severe structural failures.
Chief among these is the futile pursuit of point-level price targets. At 6-to-12 month investment horizons, capital markets are
non-stationary, fat-tailed, and dominated by unexpected macroeconomic and geopolitical shocks. Point-forecast models
generate an illusion of precision while leaving investment committees defenceless against structural regime shifts.
The Bayesian Regime Detection Engine (BRDE) re-engineers this workflow by establishing an audit-defensible, probabilistic
framework:
1. Regime Identification: Classifying current market conditions into 5 distinct economic regimes: Risk-On, Late-Cycle,
Transitional, Post-Shock, and Risk-Off;
2. Directional Probability Mass: Quantifying which market directions are gaining posterior probability mass across a
multi-model ensemble;
3. Calibrated Uncertainty Budgets: Explicitly separating reducible model disagreement (Epistemic Uncertainty) from
irreducible market stochasticity (Aleatoric Uncertainty);
4. Finite-Sample Conformal Safety: Generating adaptive prediction sets guaranteed to cover the true unobserved market
regime at nominal confidence levels (90% and 95%), even under acute distribution shifts.

1.3 Murphy Decomposition of Proper Scoring Rules (Reliability, Resolution,
Uncertainty)
In probabilistic forecasting, proper scoring rules quantify calibration and sharpness. Under the Murphy (1973) canonical
decomposition of the quadratic Brier Score, total mean squared probability error is partitioned into three orthogonal
components:
 Brier Score = Reliability - Resolution + Uncertainty
  Reliability (Calibration Error): Measures the weighted distance between forecasted probabilities and observed
 conditional event frequencies. A perfectly calibrated model achieves Reliability = 0.
 Resolution (Discrimination Ability): Measures how much conditional event probabilities differ from the unconditional
 climate frequency. Higher resolution indicates sharper, more decisive forecasts.
 Uncertainty (Inherent Stochasticity): The entropy of the base rate in the evaluation window: U = y■(1 - y■), which
 depends solely on nature and cannot be altered by model choice.

Chapter 2: Indian Equity Market Context & 5-Regime Taxonomy
2.1 Unique Structural Microstructure of the Indian Market
Quantitative models imported directly from developed Western markets invariably fail in India due to four structural
dynamics:
1. Inelastic Domestic SIP Liquidity: Systematic Investment Plans (SIP) generate over ■20,000 to ■25,000 Crores
(2.4B–3.0B USD) of automated, price-insensitive domestic liquidity every month. This constant bid truncates the duration of
bear markets but exacerbates "Late-Cycle" valuation bubbles in mid-cap and small-cap segments.
2. Foreign Institutional Investor (FII) vs Domestic Institutional Investor (DII) Antagonism: FIIs act as pro-cyclical
macro allocators sensitive to global risk appetite, USD strength, and US Treasury yields. When FIIs sell aggressively,
domestic mutual funds and insurers act as contrarian buyers, absorbing capital flight and altering classical Markovian
transition probabilities.
3. Hydrocarbon & FX Sensitivity: India imports over 85% of its crude oil requirements. A spike in Brent crude (CL=F,
BZ=F) rapidly deteriorates the Current Account Deficit, depresses the Indian Rupee (INR=X), and triggers rate hike
expectations, transmitting negative shocks to equity markets.
4. Market Breadth & Capitalization Decoupling: Large-cap indices (Nifty 50) frequently mask severe structural
drawdowns in broader markets. For example, throughout 2018, the headline Nifty 50 traded near all-time highs while the
Nifty Midcap index plunged, a classic "Late-Cycle" divergence.

3.2 Point-in-Time Publication Lag Matrix & Forward-Fill Ceilings
To prevent look-ahead bias, all data streams are aligned to the official National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) trading
calendar. Special weekend trading sessions (such as the Union Budget session on Saturday, 2025-02-01, or annual Diwali
Muhurat sessions) are explicitly purged to avoid distorting 5-day autocorrelation lags.
The following causal publication lags are enforced strictly prior to feature calculation:
  Domestic Macro Data (RBI Repo, Inflation, IIP): 1-day publication lag (t-1).
  AMFI Monthly Mutual Fund Flow & SIP Statistics: 5-day publication lag (t-5).
  EOD Market Breadth & Advance/Decline Counts: 1-day publication lag (t-1).
  Global Context (S&P 500, DXY, Brent, US Yields): 1-day lag (t-1), ensuring the foreign market session has officially
 closed before the Indian market opens at 09:15 IST.
 Controlled Forward-Fill Ceiling: Non-synchronized international holidays are filled forward with a hard limit of 3 trading
 days.
 Zero Forward-Fill on Returns: Price log returns are strictly never forward-filled; missing price bars result in zero return
 with missingness masks.

Formal Finite-Sample Coverage Theorem & Proof Sketch
Theorem (Vovk et al., 2005): If (x1, y1), , (xn+1, yn+1) are exchangeable random variables, then the prediction set C(xn+1)
satisfies:
 1 - α ≤ P≤ft( yn+1 ∈ C(xn+1) ) ≤ 1 - α + (1 / n+1)
*Proof Sketch:* By exchangeability, the nonconformity score of the test point sn+1 = 1 - p■(yn+1 xn+1) and calibration scores
s1, , sn are identically distributed. The rank of sn+1 among s1, , sn+1 follows a discrete uniform distribution on 1, , n+1.
Therefore:
 P(yn+1 ∈ C(xn+1)) = P(sn+1 ≤ q■) = ( (n+1)(1-α) / n+1) ≥ 1 - α
This guarantees distribution-free coverage validity regardless of sample size or neural network complexity.
